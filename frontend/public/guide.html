Getting started

Three things to know before your first sign-in.

Sign in at 606athletics.com
  • You'll receive an invitation email from noreply@606athletics.com. Open the link, set a password, and you're in. Nothing to download — it runs in the browser on any phone, tablet, or computer.
  • Guest coaches working a single event may instead get a 6-character access code — enter it on the app's Redeem page with your email address.
  • Forgot your password? Use Forgot password? on the sign-in page.
  • If your account has more than one role, you'll land on "Where are you working today?" — pick a workspace. You can switch anytime without signing out.
First load of the day can take 30–60 seconds. The app server naps when nobody is using it. Wait once and it stays fast. On camp days, open the app before athletes arrive so it's already awake.

Same athletes, same data — a different home screen and menu built around your job. Tap your role.

Adding players

Two ways in: one at a time through a dialog, or a whole roster at once from a spreadsheet.

Add a single player (the Add Player dialog)

Perfect for walk-ins, late registrations, or a brand-new athlete who isn't in any spreadsheet yet.

  1. Go to Athletes (or an event's Roster tab) → tap Add Player.
  2. Enter the basics: first and last name, and ideally a date of birth or graduation year — that's how the app matches this athlete to their permanent 60'6" ID at future events and avoids duplicates.
  3. Add whatever else you know: primary/secondary position, bats/throws, height, weight, team, jersey number, city and state.
  4. Add contact details if you have them — player email (for athletes old enough to have their own account) and guardian name, email, and phone. These power the platform invites later, so the more you enter now, the less typing on invite day.
  5. Save. The athlete gets a permanent 60'6" ID immediately and appears in your athlete list and any event roster you added them from.

Adding a player never sends any email by itself — invitations are a separate, deliberate step.

Add many players at once (CSV import)

If your club already keeps a roster in Excel, Google Sheets, or a registration platform, don't retype it. Export it as a CSV and import it in one pass — the importer matches existing athletes to their permanent IDs, creates new profiles for the rest, and can even drop athletes straight into event groups and assign bib numbers.

It's the fastest way to load 20, 50, or 200 athletes, and it's safe to run more than once. Everything about it — the template, every column, matching, the preview, and re-importing — is in the next section: Roster CSV import.

Roster CSV import, in depth

One spreadsheet in, a clean roster out — no duplicates, no lost data.

Start from the template (and every column it supports)

Download the official template at 606athletics.com/roster-template.csv — it has every column the importer understands, in the right order, with example rows you replace with your own athletes. You can also export from your own system as long as the column headers match.

Identity & matching (fill in as many as you can):

first_name last_name date_of_birth age graduation_year age_group

Baseball profile:

primary_position secondary_position bats throws height weight_lbs

Team & event:

team jersey_bib_number organization event_group

Contact & location (powers invites later):

player_email guardian_name guardian_email guardian_phone city state

Ignored columns — safe to leave in your file, the importer skips them:

index event_name consent_status notes

Only first and last name are truly required per row — but a date_of_birth or graduation_year is what lets the app match the athlete at every future event, so treat one of those as required too.

How matching against permanent IDs works

Every athlete has one permanent 60'6" ID. The importer's first job is to figure out whether each CSV row is an athlete you already have, or a new one:

  1. Name + date of birth — the strongest match. Same first name, last name, and DOB as an existing athlete → the row links to that athlete's permanent ID.
  2. Name + graduation year — if the row has no DOB, the importer tries first name + last name + graduation year.
  3. No match → a brand-new profile is created with a fresh permanent ID.
  4. Ambiguous — same name but the DOB or grad year doesn't line up with what's on file → the row is flagged Possible duplicate and the preview asks you to choose: link to the existing athlete or create a new one.

The system never silently creates a duplicate athlete, and never guesses a graduation year it wasn't given.

Reading the import preview (row by row)

Nothing is written until you confirm. After you pick your file, every row appears in a preview sorted into statuses:

  • Matched — links to an existing athlete's permanent ID. The event roster gets the existing athlete; blank fields on their profile are filled in from your CSV (see re-importing below).
  • New — no existing athlete found; a new profile will be created.
  • Possible duplicate — looks like someone you already have, but not certainly. Tap the row and choose: use the existing athlete, or create a separate new one.
  • Needs grad year — the row has neither a usable DOB nor a graduation year, so the app can't place the athlete in a class group. Type the grad year right there in the preview.
  • Error — the row can't be imported (for example, no name). It's skipped with a reason shown; everything else still imports.

Leave Auto-group by graduation year on (when importing into an event), then Confirm Import. Groups appear immediately with counts.

Re-importing a richer CSV — backfill without overwriting

This is the importer's most useful trick, and the one to remember when a profile looks half-empty. When a row is Matched to an existing athlete:

  • Blank fields get filled in. If the athlete's profile has no height, no guardian email, no positions — and your CSV has them — the import fills them in.
  • Existing values are never overwritten. If the profile already says the athlete bats left and your CSV says right, the profile keeps what it had. An import can only add, never replace.

So if your first import was a bare-bones list (names only) and profiles look thin: add the missing columns to your spreadsheet — emails, guardians, positions, heights — and import the same file again. Matched athletes soak up the new data; nothing you've entered by hand since is touched, and no duplicates are created.

Need to change a value that's already set (a typo'd guardian email, a wrong DOB)? Edit the athlete's profile directly — that's deliberate, so a stray spreadsheet can never trample corrections your staff made in the app.

event_group and bib numbers — pre-building your camp day

Two columns do extra work when you import into an event:

  • event_group — places each athlete straight into a group on import. Write "2032" and the athlete lands in a group named "Class of 2032" (created if it doesn't exist yet). You can also write any custom group name — "Morning Session", "Pitchers Only" — and athletes with the same value end up together.
  • jersey_bib_number — becomes the athlete's bib number for the event, so evaluators can find players by bib from the first pitch without a check-in scramble.

Rows without an event_group still get placed by Auto-group by graduation year if you leave it on.

Events — setup to teardown

Configure once, assign automatically where possible, evaluate with minimum taps.

The setup checklist
  1. Create the event — Events → New Event → name, date, location. The Overview tab then shows a setup checklist that tracks itself: Roster → Groups → Stations → Evaluators → Activate. Each step lights up as you complete it, so you always know what's left.
  2. Roster — import a CSV or add players by hand. Existing athletes match to their permanent 60'6" ID instead of being duplicated.
  3. Groups — build themselves from graduation years, or come straight from your CSV's event_group column. Adjust as needed (below).
  4. Stations — pick from the preset library and set each module Required, Optional, or Not Offered (below).
  5. Evaluators — assign Evaluator → Group → Station → Save. Each evaluator sees only their assignment. Check the staffing calculator while you're here.
  6. Activate — set status to Evaluation Active and run the day from the Live Progress tab.
Groups — auto-grouping, renaming, merging

With Auto-group by graduation year on, importing a roster builds groups like "Class of 2033", "Class of 2034"… automatically, with live counts.

  • Rename a group — open the group and edit its name. "Class of 2033" can become "13U Gold" without moving anyone.
  • Merge groups — two small classes can run as one station rotation: merge them and every athlete, assignment and count moves to the surviving group.
  • Move athletes between groups by hand, and add walk-ins directly into a group on the day — no second import needed.
Stations — presets and Required / Optional / Not Offered

Add stations from the preset library:

Athletic Movement Hitting Infield Outfield Throwing/Arm Pitching Catching Base Running Baseball IQ Character

Each module at your event is in one of three states:

  • Required — counts toward completion; evaluators are warned if it's missing at submit time.
  • Optional — scored if there's time; never blocks completion.
  • Not Offered — removed from completion math entirely. No mound? Mark Pitching Not Offered and nobody shows "missing data".

There's also an athletic testing library for measurable tests — 60-yard dash, broad jump, exit velocity and friends — so timed and measured numbers live alongside scored modules and feed the athlete's verified-measurement history.

The staffing calculator

On the Evaluators tab, the staffing panel does the math you'd otherwise do on a napkin: given your enrollment and station count, it shows three numbers — minimum (the day works, barely), recommended (comfortable rotations), and ideal (no waiting, full coverage).

It also warns about specific bottlenecks before the day — e.g. "5 stations but only 3 evaluators assigned" — so you can recruit or trim stations while it's still cheap to fix.

Run a camp day (site manager)
  1. Open the app before athletes arrive (wakes the server, caches templates).
  2. Events → your event → set status to Evaluation Active.
  3. Check-In tab: check athletes in, assign bib numbers and groups. You can also set which positions each athlete is being evaluated for today.
  4. Confirm Stations and Evaluators assignments.
  5. Watch the Live Progress tab: checked-in, in progress, complete, missing scores, videos awaiting approval. Tap any player to see exactly which metrics are missing and whether they're ready for final submission.
  6. After the event: Review Queue → approve → Reports for rankings, CSV, and player PDFs — then generate AI assessments.

Print blank paper score sheets as backup. Dead battery beats no plan.

Give a guest coach access for one event
  1. Event → Evaluators tab → enter the coach's personal email.
  2. Pick their station / player group.
  3. The app emails them a secure 6-character code (it's also shown on screen so you can text it).
  4. They sign in via the Redeem page with that code — and see only their assigned players and station. No parent contacts, no medical info, no other coaches' private notes.
  5. Access expires automatically when the event ends — or revoke it instantly from the same tab.
Deleting an event

Open the event and use the trash icon on the header (admins and owners only). Deleting removes the event, its roster links, groups, stations and evaluator assignments — athlete profiles are never touched; they keep their permanent IDs and all history from other events.

Protection: an event that already has submitted evaluations is blocked from deletion — those are permanent athlete records. Only the organization owner can force-delete such an event, and the confirmation makes clear what will be lost. Use force deletion for genuine test data only.

Evaluating on the field

Fast to tap, honest by design, and safe on stadium wifi.

The 8–12 developmental scale

Every scored metric uses a five-point 8 to 12 scale — deliberately echoing how scouts talk, but tuned for development rather than draft grades:

  • 8 — Emerging. The skill is just appearing; fundamentals are still forming.
  • 9 — Developing. Flashes of the skill; inconsistent rep to rep.
  • 10 — Established. Solid, repeatable execution for the athlete's age group.
  • 11 — Advanced. Consistently above age-group standard; stands out at the station.
  • 12 — Exceptional. Advanced well beyond age group; a genuine separator.

There's no failing grade on purpose. An 8 isn't an insult — it says "here's where the development starts," which is exactly what the goal-setting and AI assessment tools build on. Score what you saw today, not potential.

When a score doesn't apply: N/O, N/A, DNP, Retest

Never invent a number. Each metric has one-tap alternatives:

  • N/O — Not Observed. You didn't get to see it (rotation ran short, athlete was at another station).
  • N/A — Not Applicable. Doesn't apply to this athlete (catcher-only metric for a middle infielder).
  • DNP — Did Not Participate. The athlete sat out this drill (minor injury, arrived late).
  • Retest. You saw it but want another look — flags the athlete for a follow-up without blocking submission.

None of these ever count as a zero. They're excluded from the athlete's score entirely — averages are computed only over metrics that were actually scored. They also satisfy a required field, so you can finish the sheet honestly.

Observation tags — describe without typing

On the clock at a station, typing is the enemy. Tags let you record what you saw in two taps:

  • Base running tags — on running and base-running metrics, tap chips like Touched Front of Bag, Proper Turn, Broke Down Properly, Wide Turn. Enter the time → tap a tag or two → next athlete.
  • General observation tags — quick descriptors available on any metric (effort, body control, coachability moments) so patterns show up across evaluators without anyone writing an essay.

Tags ride along with the evaluation into review, reports, and the AI assessment — they're often where the best assessment sentences come from.

Autosave & offline — keep scoring when the wifi dies

Every tap saves automatically — there is no Save button to forget. And the app is built for fields with no signal:

  • Connection drops? Keep evaluating. Scores save to your device instantly and the status pill tells you the truth: Synced means the server has confirmed it; anything else means it's safely queued on the device.
  • Back in range? Everything queued syncs automatically — no button to press.
  • Even closing the browser and reopening it offline keeps your work.
  • The one thing to never do mid-event: clear the browser's site data — that's where offline work lives.
Submitting — and why submitted evaluations lock
  1. Sign in → tap Continue Evaluating on your assignment.
  2. Find the player — type their bib number (fastest) or name. Only categories relevant to that player's age and position appear; use Show all if you need the rest.
  3. Tap scores on the 8–12 scale, or N/O / N/A / DNP / Retest where honest. Add tags, a note, or a photo/video if useful.
  4. Fix anything the missing-scores warning flags, then Submit.
  5. Tap Next — it shows the next player's bib and name.

Submitting locks the evaluation. It moves into the Review Queue and you can no longer edit it — that's what makes an approved evaluation a trustworthy, permanent record. Spot a mistake after submitting? Tell your head scout or admin: they can return it to you, which unlocks it for fixes and resubmission.

Review & approval

Nothing becomes part of an athlete's permanent record — and nothing feeds an AI assessment — until a leader has looked at it.

The Review Queue

Every submitted evaluation lands in the Review Queue, visible from Organization HQ (Awaiting Review) and the head scout's Review Desk. Only three roles can approve: owner, admin, and head scout. Coaches and evaluators submit; leaders approve.

  1. Open a submitted evaluation from the queue.
  2. Tap Results summary for the visual version — score, change since last event, radar, top strengths and improvement areas — or review metric by metric.
  3. Approve — it becomes part of the athlete's permanent record, feeds reports and rankings, and becomes eligible for AI assessment.
  4. Or Return it with a note.
Returned evaluations — the fix-it loop

Returning an evaluation sends it back to the evaluator with your note ("Bib 24's arm scores look swapped with bib 42 — can you check?"). On their side:

  • The evaluation reappears in their list, unlocked, with your note on top.
  • They fix it and resubmit — it returns to the queue for a fresh look.
  • Nothing reaches the athlete's record, reports, or AI assessment until it's approved.

Returns aren't a punishment — they're how a second set of eyes keeps the permanent record clean.

AI player assessments

Approved evaluations become a written, coach-reviewed development report for each athlete — generated by AI, edited and signed off by a human, then published to the family.

The pipeline — from field to family, step by step

Every assessment moves through a fixed chain. You can always see where an athlete is:

  1. Evaluation In Progress — evaluators are still scoring at the event.
  2. Submitted — the evaluator finished and submitted; the evaluation is locked.
  3. Under Review — it's in the Review Queue awaiting an owner, admin, or head scout.
  4. Approved — a leader signed off. Only now does the athlete become eligible for an AI assessment.
  5. AI Assessment Generated — an owner or admin generated the draft from the approved evaluations.
  6. Awaiting Final Approval — the draft is being read, edited, and finished by a human.
  7. Published — released to the athlete and family, with an email notification.

Nothing is generated until evaluations are approved. The AI never sees unreviewed scores — garbage in, garbage out is designed out of the system.

Generating, editing, and regenerating (owner / admin only)

Only owners and admins can work this stage — head scouts approve evaluations but don't generate or publish assessments.

  1. After approving an event's evaluations, open the athlete (or the event's assessments view) and tap Generate AI Assessment. The AI writes a draft from the approved scores, observation tags, and notes — strengths, development areas, and next steps in plain coach language.
  2. Read the whole draft. You are the editor: fix anything that doesn't match what your staff saw, soften or sharpen wording, cut anything that doesn't belong.
  3. Edit directly in the draft — your edits are kept.
  4. Not close enough? Regenerate for a fresh draft. (Regenerating replaces the current draft, so copy out anything you want to keep first.)
  5. Add a final comment — a personal note from your organization that appears with the assessment. Families consistently say this is the part they read first.

If the Generate button says the AI isn't configured, the server's AI key isn't set up — see the FAQ.

Approve & Publish — permanent, and released to the family

When the draft reads right, tap Approve & Publish. Three things happen:

  • The assessment becomes permanent — published assessments are never edited or deleted. That's what makes them a trustworthy record a family can rely on.
  • It's released to the athlete/parent portal — it appears in My 60'6" ID for the athlete and their guardians.
  • An email notification goes out telling the family a new assessment is ready.

So publish deliberately. Read it end to end, check the athlete's name and details, add your final comment — then publish. There is no unpublish.

Future events — new assessments, never overwrites

Run another camp in October and generate again: the athlete gets a new assessment alongside the old one. Published assessments are never overwritten or replaced — each one is a timestamped snapshot, and the family sees the full series. Over seasons, that stack of assessments is the development story: August said "emerging bat speed," October says "established" — and everyone can see the line connecting them.

Inviting athletes & parents

Profiles work fine without accounts — invite families when you're ready to share results with them.

The "Invite to platform" dialog
  1. Open the player's profile → tap Invite to platform.
  2. The dialog shows the contact details on file: the athlete's email and the guardian's name, email, and phone. Owners and admins can add or fix these right in the dialog — no detour to an edit screen. Fixes are saved to the profile.
  3. The dialog tells you exactly who will be invited (based on age — see routing below) and flags what's missing: no guardian email for a young athlete, no DOB on file, and so on.
  4. Tap Send. The invite email comes from noreply@606athletics.com with a link to set a password and land in My 60'6" ID.

Example: for athlete Jordan Diaz with guardian guardian@606athletics.com on file, the dialog shows both contacts, tells you the guardian will receive the invite, and lets an admin correct a typo in that address before sending.

Age routing — who actually gets the email

The app decides who to invite from the athlete's date of birth, automatically:

  • Under 13 — or no DOB on file: the guardian only is invited. Young athletes never get their own login, and when the app can't verify age it plays it safe and treats the athlete as under 13. A guardian email is required in this case.
  • 13–17: the athlete is invited to their own account, with the guardian CC'd so parents always know what their teen was sent.
  • 18 and over: the athlete is invited directly.

If the dialog insists on a guardian email you think it shouldn't need, check the athlete's DOB — no DOB means guardian-only routing. Add the date of birth and the routing updates instantly.

Expiry & resending
  • Invites expire after 14 days. An expired link politely says so and tells the family to ask for a new one.
  • Resending replaces the pending invite — the old link stops working the moment the new one is sent, so there's never a pile of live links floating around inboxes.
  • Once the invite is accepted and the account exists, the invite is done — future sign-ins are just email + password at 606athletics.com.

My 60'6" ID — the family portal

What athletes and parents see once they accept an invite.

Inside the portal
  • The athlete's profile — photo, positions, height/weight, teams, and their permanent 60'6" ID (like 606-A337F768) that follows them across seasons and organizations. The app shows what's missing so profiles complete themselves over time.
  • Published AI assessments — every assessment your organization has published for this athlete, newest first, each with the staff's final comment. Families are emailed when a new one arrives.
  • Evaluations released to them — approved evaluation results the organization has shared, with the visual results summary: scores, change since last event, top strengths and development priorities.
  • Verified measurements & personal bests — with badges showing who verified each number.
  • Parents of athletes 13–17 see the same portal via their own guardian access; under-13 athletes are represented entirely by the guardian's account.

The portal only ever shows what's been approved and released. Drafts, internal notes, and unapproved evaluations are never visible to families.

Housekeeping & safety nets

Keeping the roster tidy — and what protects you when things go wrong.

Archiving a player

An athlete who aged out or moved on doesn't need deleting — archive them from their profile. Archived athletes:

  • Disappear from active rosters, pickers, and day-to-day lists
  • Keep every record — evaluations, measurements, assessments, media — under their permanent ID
  • Can be restored at any time if they come back; a future CSV import that matches them can also bring them back to active

Archive is the right tool basically every time you're tempted to delete a real athlete.

Danger Zone — Remove all athletes (owner only)

For cleaning up after testing — not routine housekeeping. Organization owners have Settings → Danger Zone → Remove all athletes:

  • It requires typing a confirmation phrase exactly — no accidental taps.
  • It clears athletes and their records for your organization only, keeping staff, events, stations and templates.
  • It cannot be undone from inside the app.

Typical use: you loaded practice athletes while learning the app, and now it's time to import the real roster clean.

Backups

The database is backed up automatically every night. Combined with the rules you've read elsewhere in this guide — submitted evaluations lock, published assessments are permanent, imports never overwrite, deletes are role-gated and confirmed — the system is built so that one bad tap can't erase a season. If something ever does go seriously wrong, tell your organization owner right away; the nightly backup is the safety net behind everything.

Common questions

Quick answers to the things people actually ask.

The app is stuck loading / the first login is slow

The server sleeps after ~15 quiet minutes and takes 30–60 seconds to wake. Wait once — everything after that is fast. On event days, open the app before athletes arrive.

It says "Too many attempts. Please wait a minute."

A safety limit: about 15 sign-ins per minute from one network. At an event, have people sign in as they arrive rather than everyone at once. Waiting 60 seconds clears it.

I imported my CSV but my players' info didn't fill in

Most likely your first CSV only had names, or your column headers didn't match the template. The fix is a re-import:

  1. Download the template and make sure your headers match it exactly (e.g. guardian_email, not "Parent Email").
  2. Add the missing columns — emails, guardians, positions, heights, DOBs.
  3. Import the file again. Matched athletes backfill their blank fields from the new data — and nothing already set is overwritten, so it's completely safe to re-run.

See Roster CSV import for the full column list and matching rules.

The invite dialog says a guardian email is required — why?

The athlete has no date of birth on file (or is under 13). Without a DOB the app can't verify age, so it plays it safe and routes the invite to a guardian only. Add the athlete's date of birth on their profile — or right in the invite dialog if you're an owner or admin — and the routing updates instantly: 13–17 invites the athlete with the guardian CC'd, 18+ invites the athlete directly.

The AI assessment button says the AI isn't configured

The server's AI key hasn't been set up — this is a server-side setting, not something on your device or account. Ask your organization's admin (or whoever manages your deployment) to configure it. Everything else — evaluations, review, approval — works normally in the meantime, and once the key is set you can generate assessments for any already-approved evaluations.

What happens to my scores if the wifi dies mid-evaluation?

Nothing is lost. Scores save to the device instantly and sync when connection returns — the status pill only says Synced when the server has truly confirmed it. Even closing the browser and reopening it offline keeps your work. The one thing to never do mid-event: clear the browser's site data — that's where offline work lives.

What do N/O, N/A, DNP and Retest mean — and do they hurt the score?

N/O Not Observed · N/A Not Applicable · DNP Did Not Participate · Retest needs another look. None of them count as a zero — they're excluded from the athlete's score entirely. They also satisfy a required field, so an evaluator can finish a sheet honestly instead of inventing a number. Retest additionally flags the athlete for a follow-up without blocking submission.

Our field doesn't have a mound / radar gun / cage. Does that break anything?

No — that's exactly what Required / Optional / Not Offered is for. Mark what you can't run that day as Not Offered and it's removed from completion math instead of showing up as missing data. The same evaluation standard scales from one field and one radar gun to a full facility.

Are photos and videos of kids safe here?

Yes — by design. Every file records who uploaded it and its consent status. Media of athletes under 13 is never displayed anywhere until a guardian approves it. Files marked private are staff-only regardless of consent. The public story page shows approved content only, and files are stored in private cloud storage that only the app can serve.

What does the "Verified" badge on a measurement mean?

Every number shows where it came from: Athlete Submitted Parent Submitted Coach Submitted Event Verified Device Verified 60'6" Verified

Self-reported numbers look visibly different from staff-verified ones — a college coach looking at "88 mph, Event Verified" knows a staff member measured it at a real event. The app enforces this: an athlete literally cannot mark their own number as verified.

My athlete changed teams / a new season started. Do we make a new profile?

No — never. That's the whole point of the permanent 60'6" ID. Each season stacks under the same profile with its own team, measurements, and evaluations, and the Career Overview shows the multi-year story. Old evaluations are never erased or overwritten — and each new event's AI assessment is added alongside the previous ones, never replacing them.

Why can't I see certain information (parent contacts, private notes)?

Access is role-based on purpose. Evaluators see only their assigned players and never see family contacts, medical, or financial details. Coaches don't see other coaches' private notes. Scout assessments marked confidential are visible only to organization leadership. If you think you're missing something you should have, ask your organization's admin — they control roles.

How do I switch between roles without signing out?

If your account is authorized for more than one, use the Workspace switcher in the sidebar (or the "More" menu on a phone), just under your organization name. Owners can drop into Coach Hub, Scout Mode, or Evaluation Mode and back. Your permissions never change — only the view. See Roles & workspaces.

How do I delete a test event, or clear out practice athletes?

Event: open it and use the trash icon on the header (admins only). It removes the event, its roster links, groups, stations and assignments — athlete profiles are never touched. An event with submitted evaluations is protected from deletion; only the owner can force it.

All athletes: organization owners have Settings → Danger Zone → Remove all athletes, which requires typing a confirmation phrase. It clears athletes and their records for that organization only, keeping staff, events, stations and templates. It cannot be undone — so use it for cleaning up after testing, not routine housekeeping.

Can I use it on my phone? Is there an app to install?

It's built phone-first — evaluators score on phones at the field. Nothing to install: open 606athletics.com in your browser. Tip: use your browser's Add to Home Screen to get an app-style icon.

I didn't get my invitation email

Check spam first — it comes from noreply@606athletics.com. Ask your organization admin to re-send the invite (open the player's profile → Invite to platform → Send again — resending replaces the old link). Remember invites expire after 14 days. For event access codes, the site manager can also read the code off their screen and text it to you.

Who owns the data? What happens between camps?

Your organization's data belongs to your organization, in its own walled-off space — organizations can never see each other's athletes. Everything persists between events: run a camp in August and another in October, and every athlete's history simply grows. Evaluations are permanent, timestamped records, and the database is backed up nightly.

Something looks wrong or broke — what do I do?

Note what you were doing, your role, and the screen you were on, then tell your organization admin. A hard refresh (pull down on mobile) fixes most display oddities after an update. Your scores are safe either way — saving is the first thing the app does.
