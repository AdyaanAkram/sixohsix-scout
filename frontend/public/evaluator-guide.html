Evaluator Guide

Everything you need to score athletes at a camp. Written for coaches — no computer knowledge needed.

Before you worry about breaking something: you can't.

You only ever see the stations you were assigned. You can't delete anything, you can't change another coach's scores, and every tap saves itself. If you tap the wrong answer, just tap the right one — the last tap wins.

The shape of the day

The whole job in 30 seconds

You stand at one station. Athletes come to you in groups. For each athlete you watch them do the drill, tap how it went, and move to the next one.

Sign in → open your station → tap an athlete → tap what you saw → Submit → next athlete.

That's the entire system. Everything below is just detail on those six steps.

Step 4 — the important one

Scoring an athlete

Tap an athlete and you get the scoring screen. It asks you one thing at a time. There are no numbers to remember and nothing to calculate.

Notes are optional — but a single line like "quick first step, slows through the bag" is worth a great deal to the family reading it later.

The bar at the top fills as you answer. When it's full, you've answered everything required.
Your work saves as you go

Every tap is saved the moment you make it. If your battery dies, if the app closes, if you hand the tablet to someone else — nothing is lost. Sign back in and pick up where you were.

Score what you actually saw

Don't average a kid against the group, and don't soften a score because a parent is watching. A head coach reviews everything before any family sees it, and honest scores are what make the report worth reading.

Step 5

When an athlete doesn't do the drill

Sometimes you can't score something. Rather than guessing, use one of these four marks:

N/ONot Observed
N/ANot Applicable
DNPDid Not Participate
RetestNeeds Retest
1
N/O — Not Observed. You were there but didn't get a clean look. Rotation moved too fast, you were helping another kid.
2
N/A — Not Applicable. The drill doesn't apply to this athlete. Catcher framing for a kid who never catches.
3
DNP — Did Not Participate. They sat out. Injured, left early, didn't want to.
4
Retest — Needs Retest. Something went wrong with the rep itself. Bad bounce, equipment failure, a clear do-over. This flags it so someone can run it again.
These are better than a low score

A kid who never got a fair rep should not carry a bad number for the rest of the season. Marking it honestly is the right call every time.

Step 6

Submitting

When the bar at the top is full, tap the big red Submit button at the bottom of the screen. That sends your scores to the head coach for review, and moves the athlete to Done.

After you submit, you can't edit

That's deliberate — it keeps the record trustworthy. If you got something wrong, tell the head coach. They can send it back to you in one tap, and then you can fix and resubmit.

Your finished work lives under Submitted in the left-hand menu, so you can always look back at what you scored.

Step 7

If something comes back to you

An athlete with a red Returned label means a head coach looked at your scores and wants a second pass. Their note sits in a red box at the top of the scoring screen telling you why.

Returned for revision
Please add a note on the throwing mechanics — the score alone won't tell the family much.

Fix what they asked for and tap Submit again. That's it. Being returned isn't a mark against you — it's usually a request for a bit more detail.

Reference

Questions coaches actually ask

The wifi at the field is terrible. Will this work?

Yes, if you open your station once while you still have signal. The app downloads the scoring sheets to your device the moment you open a station — you'll see a line saying templates are cached. After that you can score with no signal at all, and it sends everything up when you're back online. Open your station in the parking lot before you walk out.

I tapped the wrong answer.

Tap the right one. The last tap is what counts. Nothing is locked until you hit Submit.

I don't see any athletes in my list.

Usually the group hasn't been checked in yet — the list only shows athletes who are checked in at the event. Ask whoever is running the front table. If your whole station is missing, you haven't been assigned to it.

An athlete walked up who isn't on my list.

Search their name or bib number at the top. If they genuinely aren't there, they haven't been checked in — send them to the front table rather than scoring someone else's record.

Can I see another coach's scores?

No. You see your own work only. That's on purpose, so nobody is anchored by someone else's number.

My tablet died mid-athlete.

Nothing is lost. Sign back in, open your station, and the athlete will be sitting in "In progress" exactly where you left off.

Do parents see this straight away?

No. Every evaluation goes to a head coach first. Families only see a finished assessment after it's reviewed and published — often days later.

How long should one athlete take?

A minute or two. If you're spending much longer, you're probably overthinking it — tap what you saw and move on. Your first instinct at a station is usually the honest one.

Something is broken or looks wrong.

Tell whoever is running the event, and keep scoring — your work is saved either way. Don't try to fix data yourself.
